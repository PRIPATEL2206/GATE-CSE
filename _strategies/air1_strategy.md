# 🏆 AIR-1 Strategy

> Distilled from interviews with multiple GATE CS top-100 rankers. The patterns repeat.

---

## The Three Non-Negotiables

1. **PYQs are the syllabus.** The official syllabus is a *menu*; PYQs of the last 15 years are the *real exam*.
2. **Depth over breadth, then breadth.** Master 4–5 high-weight subjects to ~95%, *then* sweep the rest to ~80%.
3. **Speed is engineered, not gifted.** It comes from PYQ repetition + pattern recognition, not from solving harder problems.

---

## High-Weight Subjects (last 10 years average)

| Tier | Subjects | Why |
|---|---|---|
| S | DS + Algorithms, OS, DBMS, Computer Networks | Highest mark count + multi-step numericals |
| A | Engineering Mathematics, TOC, Digital Logic | Predictable + formulaic |
| B | COA, Compilers, Aptitude | Smaller but cheap if mastered |

Strategy: never let a Tier S subject score < 80% in any FLT.

---

## The 3-Pass System (per topic)

1. **Pass 1 — Concept** (Sections 1–4 of the topic file): goal = explain in your own words.
2. **Pass 2 — PYQ** (Section 5): solve every PYQ, *with timer*. The first time will be slow — that's expected.
3. **Pass 3 — Pattern** (Sections 6–8): solve practice + add pattern entries. You should now *recognize* the question type before reading it fully.

A topic is "done" only after Pass 3.

---

## What Top Rankers Do Differently

- **They re-do PYQs 3+ times.** Not once. Three times. Every time, faster.
- **They keep one notebook.** Not 11. One book of formulas + tricks + mistakes — they re-read it weekly.
- **They take FLTs in real conditions.** Same start time. No phone. No water break. Computer.
- **They review tests longer than they take them.** A 3-hr FLT → 4–5 hr review.
- **They don't chase new resources.** One source per subject. Mastery > coverage.

---

## What Top Rankers *Don't* Do

- Watch lectures passively.
- Take notes in pretty handwriting (waste — type, search, re-use).
- Solve problems without a timer.
- Save weak subjects for the end ("I'll do Compilers in March").
- Skip aptitude ("it's easy").

---

## The 1% Tactics

1. **Marker-pen rule:** every PYQ you got right but felt unsure about → marked → re-do in 7 days.
2. **Negative-marking math:** GATE has 1/3 negative on MCQs. **Skip iff confidence < 50%.** NATs have no negative — always attempt.
3. **First 30 minutes** of the exam = aptitude + fast 1-mark questions. Build momentum + buffer.
4. **Flag-and-move:** any question you can't solve in ~2 min on first pass — mark and skip. Come back fresh.
5. **Last 5 minutes:** revisit only the marked NATs. Never start a new question.

---

## Mental Model

You are not preparing for an exam. You are training a **pattern-recognition system** to map a question to a solution template in < 30 seconds. Every PYQ you redo strengthens that map. Every mistakes-log entry adds an exception handler.

The exam day is the deployment — not the training.
